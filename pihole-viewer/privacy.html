Privacy Policy — Pi-hole Viewer

Last updated: 7 November 2026

Short version: everything the app knows lives on your phone, and the only thing it ever talks to is the Pi-hole you point it at. I don't collect anything, because I never receive anything. The longer version is below, because you deserve to know exactly what that means.

This policy covers the Pi-hole Viewer Android app. The Field Service Sidekick privacy policy covers the agent service, which is a different product and handles different things.

1. What the app stores

All of it sits on your device, and there are three things:

  • the address of each Pi-hole you add, and the name you give it;
  • the password you use to sign in to that Pi-hole's web admin page — the same password you'd type into a browser. Pi-hole doesn't issue separate API keys, so this is the real one;
  • a certificate fingerprint, if you've chosen to trust a self-signed certificate for that Pi-hole.

The password is encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a 256-bit key held in your phone's Android Keystore. That key is generated on your device and cannot be exported from it — not by me, not by anything else.

2. Where that information goes

From your phone straight to the Pi-hole address you configured. Nothing passes through me. There's no account to create, no sign-in with me, and no server of mine anywhere in the path.

The app is an administration client. It signs in to your Pi-hole and reads and writes over its API — so when you toggle blocking or add a blocklist entry, that goes to your Pi-hole and nowhere else.

If you reach your Pi-hole at a name that resolves outside your home network — a hostname, a VPN address, a public URL — that's still your address and your server. It's the one you typed in.

3. What the app doesn't do

  • It doesn't send anything to me, or to any third party.
  • It contains no analytics, no crash-reporting, no advertising, and no tracking SDK of any kind.
  • It doesn't ask for your name, your email, or any account of yours.
  • It doesn't collect device identifiers, location, contacts, or files.
  • It doesn't run in the background, and it doesn't contact anything you haven't configured yourself.

One honest exception, and it isn't the app doing it: Google Play can send a developer crash and diagnostics reports for apps you install, if you've opted into sharing that with developers in your Android settings. That's a Play setting and Google's data, not something this app collects or sends.

4. Permissions

Internet access, and that's the only permission the app requests. It needs it to reach your Pi-hole. There is no location, camera, contacts, storage, or notification access.

5. Certificates, and plain HTTP

A Pi-hole serves its admin page over HTTPS with a certificate it generates itself, which no phone will trust automatically. The app doesn't quietly accept that. It shows you the certificate's fingerprint and asks whether to trust that exact certificate before it will connect. If your Pi-hole later regenerates its certificate, the fingerprint stops matching and you'll be asked to confirm the new one.

If you enter an http:// address for a Pi-hole, the traffic between your phone and that Pi-hole is unencrypted. Use HTTPS unless you have a specific reason not to.

6. Your phone's backup

Android can back up app data to your Google account, and can carry it across when you move to a new phone. The app excludes what it stores from both. That's deliberate rather than incidental: the encryption key protecting your password can't be backed up, so a restored copy would be unreadable anyway. Your Pi-hole addresses and passwords don't leave your device that way either.

7. Deleting what the app holds

Delete a Pi-hole inside the app, or uninstall the app. Either removes everything it stored. You don't need to ask me for an export or ask me to delete anything — I never had a copy.

8. Children

The app is an administration tool for a self-hosted DNS server. It isn't directed at children and it isn't designed to be used by them.

9. Changes to this policy

If anything here changes, the date at the top changes with it. Since the app collects nothing, there's very little that can change.
